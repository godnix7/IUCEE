Urban areas face major challenges in managing and planning civic infrastructure due to the lack of accurate, up-to-date, and centralized data. Information about essential facilities such as roads, hospitals, schools, water supply, and slum areas is often collected manually, making it time-consuming, inconsistent, and difficult to scale.
As cities grow rapidly, authorities struggle to evaluate whether infrastructure is sufficient for the population. There is no efficient system to measure infrastructure availability per unit population (e.g., per 1000 people), which leads to poor planning, unequal resource distribution, and delayed decision-making.
Additionally, existing systems do not effectively utilize modern data sources such as satellite imagery and drone data, which can provide real-time insights.
Therefore, there is a need for an intelligent system that can automatically identify civic infrastructure from Geo-spatial data and provide meaningful, data-driven benchmarks to support better urban planning and governance.
